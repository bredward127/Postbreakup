# The "Where It Lands" Audit

Source: *The Starter Dose* (ClickDose). Ten checks to run on any ad and page pair before a dollar of spend touches it. Score each one pass or fail (or *unknown* when you lack the input, e.g. no ad copy). **Two or more fails means fix the page before you fix the ad.**

| # | Check | Pass when |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **The promise survives the click.** | The exact promise (and nickname) from the ad appears on the page's first screen, in the same words. |
| 02 | **The belief split is deliberate.** | You can say in one sentence which half (ad or page) does the convincing. If both do it, the visitor reads the pitch twice. If neither does, nobody buys. |
| 03 | **Traffic temperature matches format.** | Cold traffic lands on a belief-builder (advertorial, listicle). Pre-sold traffic lands on a closer. See the blueprint chart. |
| 04 | **Five-second test.** | A stranger can answer "what is this, who's it for, what do I get" from one screen without scrolling. |
| 05 | **Every claim has proof within one scroll.** | A stat, review, guarantee, or mechanism sits next to each promise. Orphan claims read as hype. |
| 06 | **The mechanism is stated.** | The page says WHY the product works, in plain words. Benefits without a *because* don't survive skeptics. |
| 07 | **One conversion goal.** | One primary action, repeated. Nav links, social icons, and second offers are exits wearing costumes. |
| 08 | **The guarantee kills the biggest fear.** | The refund terms answer the actual objection ("what if it doesn't work for ME"), and there's a reason you can afford it. |
| 09 | **Mobile is the page.** | You checked the page on a phone, where the traffic actually is, not just on the designer's monitor. |
| 10 | **You can name the test.** | You know what challenger this page will be tested against and what number decides. A page without a planned test is a guess with a URL. |

Report it as a table: `# | Check | Result | Why (one line)`. Never mark a check "pass" you couldn't verify.

For check 08: never write a guarantee the owner hasn't approved. If there isn't one, mark fail and ask for terms.

## The Belief Split

Your ad and your page are one pitch, split in two. If unsure where to start, pick one of these and run it first.

### Split A: Long ad, short page
The caption is the advertorial: story, mechanism and proof, all before the click. The page is a pure closer (Blueprint 4, Offer Page): offer, guarantee, buy button.
- **Best when:** the story is strong, the product needs explaining, and the audience reads (older demographics, considered purchases).
- **Watch for:** expensive clicks. Everyone who clicks is nearly sold, but fewer click.

### Split B: Short ad, long page
The ad opens a loop (hook, nickname, curiosity) and hands off fast. The page does the convincing: advertorial or listicle first, offer second.
- **Best when:** you want cheap clicks at volume and the page can carry the argument.
- **Watch for:** cheap clicks that bounce. The page inherits all the belief-building work.

**Try this first:** pick one split on purpose, write both halves in the same sitting, then test the other split against it. The ad finds the customer; the page gets tested until it stops leaking money.
