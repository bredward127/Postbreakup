# The Page Blueprints

Source: *The Starter Dose* (ClickDose). The kit gives each format's audience and job; it lists the full section order for the Listicle only. The other section orders below are working anatomies built from those descriptions and from ClickDose's live example pages (e.g. the Sable sales page). Treat them as strong defaults, not law.

## Pick your blueprint

First decide: **does the ad convince, or does the page?** That answer picks the blueprint.

| Situation | Blueprint |
|---|---|
| Cold traffic, and they read | 1. Story Advertorial |
| Skeptical audience, burned by hype before | 2. Founder-Letter Advertorial |
| Cold traffic, and they skim | 3. Listicle |
| Your ad caption already sells | 4. Offer Page |
| Service lead gen, form or call | 5. Lead Gen Page |
| Warm or retargeting traffic | 6. Sales Page |
| You're up against a named alternative | 7. Comparison Page |

---

## 1. The Story Advertorial
**Cold traffic · problem-aware.** An editorial story that sells before the reader knows they're being sold.

Working anatomy:
1. Editorial masthead (publication feel, byline, date, read time)
2. Headline about a person and a discovery, not the product
3. The story: the struggle, what failed, the turning point
4. The mechanism: why the old ways failed and what actually works, in plain words
5. Product introduced as the thing that delivers the mechanism
6. Proof (results, reviews, stats)
7. Offer + urgency/reason to act now + CTA
8. Comments or reader reactions (real only)

Killer mistake: revealing the product in the first screen. It stops reading like editorial.

## 2. The Founder-Letter Advertorial
**Skeptical audiences · burned buyers.** A first-person letter built on radical candor: what the alternatives do well, what this product won't do, the real math.

Working anatomy:
1. "A letter from [founder]" header with photo and signature
2. Opening admission: why the reader is right to be skeptical
3. What the alternatives do well (honestly)
4. Where they fall short, and why the founder built this
5. What this product will *not* do
6. The real math (cost per use, time, comparison)
7. Proof + guarantee
8. Signed close + CTA

Killer mistake: fake humility. The candor has to cost something (a real limitation, a real concession).

## 3. The Listicle
**Cold traffic · skimmers.** "N Things Your ___ Isn't Doing." The fast, skimmable belief-builder for people who will not read a story. Each item teaches one piece of your mechanism and quietly disqualifies an alternative.

Anatomy, in order (from the kit):
1. Editorial masthead (publication feel)
2. Numbered headline aimed at what they already use
3. Items 1–3: teach mechanism, disqualify rivals
4. Items 4–5: introduce the product as the fix
5. Proof block
6. Offer stack + CTA

Killer mistake: listing features instead of teaching. Every item must make the reader smarter about their problem. Features go on the product page.

## 4. The Offer Page
**Pre-sold traffic · post-presell.** The closer. When your ad caption or presell already did the convincing, this page has one job: take the order.

Working anatomy:
1. Headline that repeats the ad's promise in the same words
2. Product image + price + primary CTA above the fold
3. Offer stack (tiers/bundles, what's included, savings)
4. Guarantee
5. Short proof strip
6. FAQ answering purchase objections (shipping, returns, billing)
7. CTA repeated + sticky mobile bar

Killer mistake: re-selling. Long argument on a closer makes pre-sold visitors hesitate.

## 5. The Lead Gen Page
**High-intent services · urgent problems.** For offers where the conversion is a form or a call.

Working anatomy:
1. Headline naming the problem and the outcome, plus location/service area if local
2. Form or call button above the fold (short form, 3–5 fields)
3. Trust bar (licenses, years, ratings, insurers, logos: real only)
4. How it works in 3 steps
5. Proof (reviews, before/after, case results)
6. What happens after you submit (response time, who calls)
7. FAQ + form repeated

Killer mistake: a long form up top. Ask for the minimum; qualify on the call.

## 6. The Sales Page
**Warm traffic · considered purchases.** The full argument, in one scroll.

Working anatomy (from the Sable example):
1. Top announcement bar (shipping/offer)
2. Header: wordmark + primary CTA button
3. Hero: rating/eyebrow, headline with an italic accent line, one-line lede, primary CTA, 2–3 micro-assurances, product image/video
4. Scrolling ticker of key claims
5. Big stat or quote card
6. "The real problem": what's actually going on, with a highlighted callout
7. The mechanism / "what's inside": image + 3 numbered components (phases, ingredients, themes)
8. The ritual / how it works: 3 numbered steps
9. Results: 3 stat circles (real data only)
10. Comparison table vs the other options (dark section)
11. Testimonials (real only)
12. Guarantee band (dark) + CTA
13. Offer box: product image, tier selector, what's included, CTA
14. FAQ: "Fair questions about a big claim"
15. Footer + sticky mobile CTA bar

Killer mistake: a hero that's poetic instead of clear. It fails the five-second test.

## 7. The Comparison Page
**Solution-aware · "X vs Y" searchers.** For the buyer deciding between you and a named alternative.

Working anatomy:
1. Headline: "[Us] vs [Them]: an honest comparison"
2. Quick verdict: who each option is best for
3. Comparison table (price, features, terms: verifiable facts only)
4. Where the alternative wins (credibility)
5. Where you win, with proof
6. Switching: how easy it is, and what carries over
7. Offer + CTA

Killer mistake: a table where you win every row. Nobody believes it.

## Other page types (from the ClickDose gallery)

Map these to the closest blueprint above and adapt:

| Page type | Build from | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Homepages / Brand Sites | Sales Page | Broader: navigation allowed, several products, brand story. Still one primary CTA per screen. |
| Advertorials | 1 or 2 | Story vs founder letter, by audience skepticism. |
| Five-Reasons Pages | Listicle | Fixed N=5, each reason one belief. |
| Offer Pages / Entry Product Pages | Offer Page | Entry product = low-price first purchase; keep the page short. |
| Quiz | Lead Gen | Quiz questions replace the form; results page is an Offer Page. |
| Switcher Pages | Comparison | Aimed at users of a named competitor; emphasize switching ease. |
| Pain Point Pages | Story Advertorial | One pain, one page; headline is the pain in the customer's words. |
| Compare Pages | Comparison | |
| Product Launches | Sales Page | Add launch date, waitlist/preorder, and what's new. |
| Product Pages | Offer Page + proof | Gallery, variants, reviews, details, shipping. |
| Landing Pages | Pick by traffic temperature | |
| Second-Half Pages | Offer Page | The page after a presell; picks up exactly where the presell ended. |
| Live Demo Pages | Sales Page | Replace the mechanism section with an interactive or video demo. |
| Parody Product Pages | Any | Humor format; still one CTA and a real offer. |
